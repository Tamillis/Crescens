# Weird Suburbia

Weird Suburbia is a module aimed at recreating that feel of 80s/90s kids and teens investigating a horror in the more intimate scale of their small-town or suburb: one where the adults just don't seem to be helping. It hopes to allow for games like Stranger Things, Tales from the Loop or even the Goonies.

## Character Creation
As normal pick an 'Ancestry' and 'Background', with Weird Suburbia replacing these terms with 'Age' and 'Stereotype' respectively.

As before your 'Role' you determine yourself, choosing 2 Powers as appropriate, with the added option of Weird Powers (see below).

## Age
Before character creation, the group should discuss this together with their GM:

> Will all the Adults be part of this adventure, or barriers to it?

Most Weird Suburbia games work best with a group of Kids or Teens. Adults as player characters are possible but change the tone of the game, so make sure everyone is on-board with that first.

### Kid
Roughly 9-13 years old. Small, overlooked, and surprisingly hard to break. Adults and Teens rarely take Kids seriously. This is usually a problem, but occasionally it is exactly what is needed.

**Young** - Kids roll Statistics differently to normal: a 1-2 is a d4 and 3-6 is a d6, and no Stat can exceed d8 through play. Additionally, they pick Skills with a total of 6 ranks, and a skill cannot start above rank 2. Children are underdeveloped.

**Fortune** - What they lack in capability they make up for in fortune: any initial die roll of 1 is re-rolled, keeping the new result.

### Teen
Roughly 14-18 years old, they're busy learning their place in the world and establishing their own hierarchy, trends and social connections.

Physically, teens are like Adults, and use standard character creation with no Stat restrictions. They are physically capable but caught between worlds - not small enough to be invisible, not old enough to be believed. 

**Angst** - Teens are caught between two worlds in a body developing faster than their mind, and have to take it out on the world. They can channel their angst to relieve d6 Stress damage or d6 Social damage per Rest.

### Adult
Adults use standard character creation. They're wise to the world (or think they are), and have been around enough to have learned a thing or two. They also have access: to cars, to money, to the respect of society. 

**Normal** - Adults cannot take a Weird Power at character creation - the world stopped whispering to them some time ago.

**Skilled** - Adults start the game with a Skill Increase

## Suburban Skills

Stats are unchanged.

Skills have been reframed for the Weird Suburban setting and a more child-focused lens, with one new one: Clique.

Additionally, the lowered emphasis on combat means Martial Arts, Shooting and Weaponry have been rolled into a singular "Fighting" skill. Speak with your GM if you wish to make fighting a larger part of the game, and to keep these distinctions.

- **Animals** - was Animal Handling
- **Art**
- **Convince** - was Charm
- **Tinker** - was Crafting
- **Lie** - was Deception
- **First Aid**
- **Clique** - a social skill, the skill at navigating the many subgroups that kids and teens create
- **Suss** - was Insight

## Damage and Recovery

### Damage
Characters in Weird Suburbia are hurt in three ways: physically, psychologically, and socially. All three use the same Injury system as Crescens Core - a descriptor and a rank, with ranks subtracting from relevant rolls and accumulating toward the Knock Out Limit.
What distinguishes them is not mechanics but fiction, and how they are recovered. When marking an Injury on your sheet, note it in a different colour depending on its nature:

**Hurt** - physical damage. Scraped knees, a twisted ankle, a black eye, exhaustion from running all night.

**Stressed** - psychological damage. The weight of seeing something that shouldn't exist, fear that doesn't go away, dread that follows you into daylight.

**Social** - relational and authority damage. Getting caught, being grounded, a friendship fracturing, an adult who is now paying attention to you.

The colour is never ambiguous - it is always obvious from what caused it. If a Foe inflicts a specific type of damage, it will say so.
All three colours fill the same bar. A character who is two ranks Hurt and three ranks Stressed is five ranks toward their Knock Out Limit regardless of the mix. A kid ground down by fear and social pressure might go down from a physical hit that would otherwise barely register.

### Knock Out
When total Injury ranks meet or exceed the Knock Out Limit, the character is Knocked Out. What that looks like depends on what pushed them over:

Predominantly Hurt: they physically cannot continue - a twisted ankle in the tunnel, too winded to run.

Predominantly Stressed: they freeze, flee, or break down - unable to engage with the Weird until a Play recovery scene clears enough Stressed damage.

Predominantly Social: the adults have won for now - grounded, collected, removed from the situation entirely.

The GM and player read the colour of the bar and narrate accordingly.

### Recovery
Recovery happens through scenes, not just sleep. Each type of damage has a scene that addresses it.
A proper recovery scene - one that genuinely fits the fiction, given enough time and commitment - earns a full recovery roll. If a scene is interrupted, rushed, or only partial (sleeping badly in a hedge, five minutes of distracted homework), the GM may step the relevant Statistic die one step down the chain for that roll. No new rule needed: it is simply recovery at a disadvantage.
If a character has mixed damage and goes out of their way to achieve all three kinds of recovery scene across a day, they may make all three recovery rolls.

Rest
Recovers: Hurt
A full night's sleep in a safe place. Your own bed, a friend's sofa, somewhere genuinely restful.
Roll Strength. Reduce all Hurt Injuries by the result, distributed however makes sense across those injuries.

Play
Recovers: Stressed
Doing something genuinely fun. A bike ride through the woods, an afternoon of video games, a sleepover that gets silly, a game of make-believe that runs longer than anyone expected. It has to be real play - not a distraction, not going through the motions.
Roll Agility or Intelligence depending on the nature of the play. Reduce all Stressed Injuries by the result.

Everyday Activity
Recovers: Social
Being a normal kid for a bit. Doing homework, helping with dinner, showing up where you were supposed to be, having a real conversation with a parent. Demonstrating, at least for an afternoon, that everything is fine.
Any Statistic may apply, with an argument. Intelligence for actually completing the work properly; Spirit for a genuine reconnection with family; Agility for helping out physically; Strength for the stubborn effort of keeping it together; Perception for noticing what the adults actually need and providing it.
Roll the chosen Statistic. Reduce all Social Injuries by the result.

### Lasting Hurt & Trauma

When a character is Knocked Out, roll a d20 after the scene resolves.

1-5 - Lasting Hurt - a physical mark that stays. A scar, a dodgy knee, a chipped tooth. Note it on your sheet. The GM may call on it when genuinely relevant - treat it as a rank 1 Hurt injury for that roll only.

6-14 - Lasting Trauma - something left a mark. With the GM, define a trigger: a specific thing, place, or situation. When you encounter it, take 1 Stressed injury. For longer campaigns, the GM may instead assign a persistent rank 1 Stressed injury that scene recovery can reduce but never fully clear until a significant story moment addresses it directly.

15+ - Lucky - shaken, maybe a little changed, but nothing that sticks.

### New Baggage
A Knock Out that was predominantly Social doesn't roll on the table above. Instead, the GM adds one new Baggage to the character reflecting what the adults now know, suspect, or have decided. Work it out together from the fiction.

## Baggage

Baggage is the mundane world refusing to let go. It is the curfew when you're three streets from the answer, the coach expecting you at practice when something impossible is happening in the woods, the parent who's started asking questions at exactly the wrong moment.
Every character starts with at least one Baggage from their Background. More accumulate through play - and occasionally get resolved.
How Baggage Works
Baggage is primarily fictional. The GM looks at the party's Baggage before a session and asks: which of these can I pull on today? Not every Baggage fires every session, but they should fire regularly enough to matter.
When Baggage bites, it does one or more of the following depending on severity:

Creates a situation - the most common expression. The bully appears. The curfew looms. An adult asks a direct question at the worst possible moment.
Imposes a Disadvantage - on rolls directly complicated by the Baggage. Trying to investigate while your coach is looking for you; trying to convince your friend while your reputation is working against you.
Deals Social damage directly - when the situation has already gone badly and consequences land. Getting caught. Being reported. A relationship breaking openly.

Active and Passive Baggage
Active Baggage sits quietly until triggered by circumstances. It does nothing until the situation arises, then lands hard.

Curfew is Active - irrelevant at noon, critical at 9:58pm.

Passive Baggage is a constant low hum. It doesn't need a trigger; it colours everything and the GM can reach for it at any time.

The Reputation is Passive - the troublemaker is always being watched, not just when they're actually in trouble.

### Resolving Baggage
Some Baggage can be resolved through play - a confronted bully, a repaired friendship, a parent finally brought onside. When the fiction genuinely earns it, the Baggage is crossed off.
Other Baggage is a permanent fixture of a kid's life. A struggling parent doesn't get resolved in one adventure. Mark these as Permanent - they can evolve, deepen, or occasionally ease, but they don't go away.

### Making Your Own Baggage
Any obligation, relationship, or complication that pulls the mundane world into the adventure can be Baggage. When creating your own, decide:

Is it Active or Passive?
What situation does it create when it fires?
Does it deal Social damage, impose a Disadvantage, or both?
Can it be resolved, and if so how? Or is it Permanent?

## Weird Powers
Powers
Characters in Weird Suburbia use the same Power system as Crescens Core, with one addition: the category of Weird Powers.
A Weird Power is any Power that touches something beyond the mundane - intuitions that shouldn't be possible, senses that reach too far, connections to things that shouldn't connect. They are marked with an asterisk (*). Telepathy from Core is a Weird Power. Powers from other modules may be Weird Powers. The ones in this section marked with one are too.
Kids and Teens start with one Weird Power slot at character creation. They may take a non-Weird Power instead, or leave it empty. Adults do not start with a Weird Power slot - the world stopped being strange at them some time ago. An Adult who acquires a Weird Power through play does so because the weird forced its way back in. That is always a story worth telling.
Not every Kid or Teen needs a Weird Power, and not every group needs one each. A table where only one character has something genuinely inexplicable can be just as compelling as one where everyone does. Talk to your GM.
